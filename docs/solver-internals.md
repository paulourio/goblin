# Solver Internals and Mathematical Specification

The `goblin` engine combines a revised dual and two-phase primal simplex LP solver ([`lu.go`](../lu.go), [`simplex.go`](../simplex.go)) with a branch-and-cut MILP framework ([`presolve.go`](../presolve.go), [`cuts.go`](../cuts.go), [`heuristics.go`](../heuristics.go), [`bab.go`](../bab.go)). Each section below states the governing equations and numerical invariants maintained across those subsystems.

---

## 1. Three-Stage Basis Factorization and PFI Eta Updates (`lu.go`)

The LP solver maintains a nonsingular basis $B \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times m}$ whose columns are drawn from the $n$ structural columns and the $m$ unit slack columns.

### Singleton Peeling and Sparse Markowitz LU
The sparse factorizer [`FactorizeSparse`](../lu.go) permutes $B$ into the block form

$$P B Q = \begin{pmatrix} U_{11} & B_{12} & B_{13} \\ 0 & L_{22} & 0 \\ 0 & B_{32} & N_{33} \end{pmatrix}$$

1. **Stage 1, column singletons** (positions $0 \dots s_1 - 1$): preserves all nonzero input basis entries without drop filtering and repeatedly pivots on a column with one nonzero among the remaining rows (`|pivVal| >= 1e-11`, `luPivotTol`). Those columns have no entries in later rows, so $U_{11}$ is upper triangular and $L_{11} = I$.
2. **Stage 2, row singletons** (positions $s_1 \dots s_2 - 1$): repeatedly pivots on a row with one remaining column whose entry satisfies both the floor $|p| \ge 10^{-11}$ (`luPivotTol`) and the column threshold $|p| \ge 0.1 \max_i |B_{i,q}|$. Because the block to the right of $L_{22}$ is zero, factoring $\begin{pmatrix} L_{22} \\ B_{32} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \hat{L}_{22} \\ \hat{L}_{32} \end{pmatrix} D_2$ places the unit multipliers in $\hat{L}$ and the pivot values $D_2$ on the diagonal of $U_{22}$.
3. **Stage 3, sparse Markowitz nucleus** ($n_{\text{nuc}} = m - s_2$): [`factorizeNucleusSparse`](../lu.go) maintains active sparse rows (`nucRowCol`/`nucRowVal`) and pattern-only columns (`nucColRow`) inside flat elbow-room arenas with intrusive `int32` degree buckets (`rowHead`, `colHead`) and cached column maxima (`nucColMax`). At each step it checks for a threshold-stable column singleton, a row singleton (skipping full-row `nucRowMax` rescans inside `findRowVal` and exiting `refreshNucColMax` early once the monotonic upper bound `oldMax` is attained), or the entry minimizing the Markowitz product $(r_i - 1)(c_j - 1)$ with sparsest-row-first column probing among candidates satisfying $|u_{ij}| \ge 0.1 \max_r |u_{rj}|$ and $|u_{ij}| \ge 10^{-11}$, then updates the active submatrix by sparse outer-product Gaussian steps (dropping only near-exact additive cancellations relative to `luCancelRelTol`).

Once factored, all three stages are assembled into unified pre-permuted `int32`-indexed CSR and CSC factors (`lRowPtr`/`lColPtr` for unit lower-triangular $L$, `uRowPtr`/`uColPtr` and `uInvDiag` for upper-triangular $U$) indexed directly in pivot order $0 \dots m-1$.

### `FTRAN`, `FTRANExact`, `BTRAN`, `BTRANExact`, and Hyper-Sparse Solves
- **`FTRAN` ($B x = b$), `FTRANExact`, `FTRANSparse`, `FTRANSparseHint`, and `FTRANSparseFrom`**: permutes $b$ into pivot order while recording the two-sided active nonzero window `[minNZ, lastNZ]`, solves $L y = P b$ by column-wise zero-skipping forward sweeps (`lColPtr`), solves $U z = y$ by backward column sweep down to `minNZ` (`uColPtr` and `uInvDiag`), unpermutes $x = Q z$ over `minNZ..lastNZ`, and applies the pointer-free PFI eta file in order (`FTRANExact` executes the same forward and eta sweeps without `luDropTol` intermediate truncation for primal evaluations and primal simplex directions). When the caller supplies input support `inIdx` with $|`inIdx`| < m/4$, `FTRANSparseHint` (and out-of-place `FTRANSparseFrom`) gathers inputs through `invPerm`, runs EMA-gated iterative Gilbert-Peierls reach DFS (`reachDAG`) when output density stays below 8%, and compacts output nonzeros exceeding `1e-13` (`luSolveDropTol`) directly into `fColIdx` without scanning all $m$ rows.
- **`BTRAN` ($B^T \pi = c$), `BTRANSparse`, `BTRANSparseHint`, and `BTRANExact`**: applies the PFI eta file in reverse with disjoint-range skipping (`minIdx`/`maxIdx`), permutes into column-pivot order while recording `[minNZ, lastNZ]`, solves $U^T z = Q^T c$ by forward row sweep (`uRowPtr`, `uColIdx`, `uRowVal`) or EMA-gated Gilbert-Peierls reach (`reachDAG`), solves $L^T w = z$ by backward row sweep down to `minNZ` (`lRowPtr`), and unpermutes $\pi = P^T w$ over `minNZ..lastNZ`. When $|`inIdx`| < m/4$, `BTRANSparseHint` gathers inputs through `invColPerm` and collects nonzeros exceeding `1e-13` (`luDropTol`) into `rhoIdx` without $O(m)$ vector scans. For dual multiplier refreshes, GMI cut rows, and Farkas infeasibility verification on ill-conditioned bases, [`BTRANExact`](../lu.go) executes the transpose triangular sweep with zero intermediate drop truncation.
- **Updates**: [`PushEtaSparse`](../lu.go) appends a pointer-free `int32` product-form (PFI) eta column directly from `fColIdx`, storing its bounding box `[minIdx, maxIdx]` for range-skipped `BTRAN`. After `maxEtaUpdates = 56` updates, or when a pivot fails, the basis is refactored.

## 2. Revised Dual and Two-Phase Primal Simplex (`simplex.go`)

Each row $i$ becomes $a_i^T x + s_i = b_i$ with slack bounds $s_i \in [0, \infty)$ for `LessEqual`, $(-\infty, 0]$ for `GreaterEqual`, and $[0, 0]$ for `Equal`. Cutting planes are stored as extra `LessEqual` rows.

### Conditional Power-of-Two Ruiz Scaling and Feasibility Tolerances
When every non-empty row and column norm lies inside $[10^{-4}, 10^{6}]$, the minimum nonzero magnitude is at least $10^{-4}$ with intra-matrix dynamic range at most $10^{10}$, and nonzero objective magnitudes lie inside $[10^{-5}, 10^{6}]$ with dynamic range at most $10^{11}$, `LPSolver` runs directly on the unscaled matrix. Outside those bounds, `buildScaledLP` runs four passes of power-of-two Ruiz geometric equilibration ($r_i = 2^{e_i}, c_j = 2^{f_j} \in [2^{-26}, 2^{26}]$, plus power-of-two objective scaling $\sigma_{\text{obj}}$) so mantissa bits suffer zero scaling roundoff, setting the effective dual tolerance `effDualTol = max(1e-7 / max(1, objScale), 1e-13)`. A basic variable $x_k$ is primal infeasible when it leaves $[l_k, u_k]$ by more than its scaled tolerance: slack columns use $\epsilon_p \min(1, r_i)$ with $\epsilon_p = 10^{-7}$, and structural columns use $\epsilon_p / (c_k \max(1, \max_i |a_{ik}|))$, floored at $10^{-15}$.

### Dual-First Dispatch and Equality Crash Basis
Nonbasic structurals start at the bound favored by their cost, initially paired with the slack basis $B = I$. When `numConstraints >= 8` and no dynamic cuts are attached, [`InitCrashBasis`](../simplex.go) invokes [`crashEqualityBasis`](../simplex.go) using zero-allocation `Workspace` buffers (`crashEqRows`, `crashRowDone`, `crashRowLoad`) to replace artificial equality slacks with a lower-triangular, dual-feasible set of structural columns: Pass 1 crashes zero-cost structural columns ($y = 0$ identically), and Pass 2 crashes minimum $c_j / a_{ij}$ columns on disjoint positive-coefficient equality rows while updating analytical dual steepest-edge weights $w_r \leftarrow w_r + (a_{rj} / a_{pj})^2$. To avoid displacing unit artificial slacks with dense columns that expand the Markowitz nucleus, `crashEqualityBasis` skips columns incident to dense coupling rows (`> 128` nonzeros) and requires at least `20` equality rows before crashing columns of degree `> 4`. [`LPSolver.Solve`](../simplex.go) aligns nonbasic bounds via `stabilizeNonBasicBounds` and enters [`runDualSimplexLoop`](../simplex.go) when the starting basis is dual-ready; `isDualFeasible` checks termination dual optimality (both `effDualTol` and bound-weighted $|d_j| (u_j - l_j) \le \epsilon_p$ on finite-range nonbasics) before `finalizeOptimalResult` validates original-space primal row residuals (caching residuals in `evalRowRes` and computing directional tolerances via `evalSparseRowDirTol`, with a certified unrefined fast path in `dualResidualsWithinTolWithRowRes`). Error-free transformations (`twoProd`, `twoSum`) inside `dot2Acc` and `wideDotAcc` enforce explicit `float64(...)` rounding barriers around `math.FMA` so compensated summation remains bit-exact under `GOAMD64=v3` and `arm64`. Otherwise, or if the dual loop ends without a verified optimum or Farkas certificate, it runs [`solveTwoPhasePrimalWithLimit`](../simplex.go).

### Dual Simplex
1. **Leaving row**: [`SelectDualLeaveRow64With`](../internal/simd/kernels_amd64.go) scans contiguous `xb`, `basicLB`, `basicUB`, `basicTol`, and `dse` arrays in register (AVX2 for $m \ge 32$, AVX-512 for $m \ge 2048$) to select the infeasible basic row maximizing $\text{infeas}_r^2 / w_r$, with dual steepest-edge weights $w_r$ updated over `fColIdx` by the Forrest-Goldfarb recurrence (`FTRANSparseHint(tau, rhoIdx, tauIdx)` is skipped when `len(fColIdx) <= 1`, and `tau` is cleared sparsely via `tauSparse`) and clamped to $[10^{-4}, 10^{12}]$. Appending cut rows keeps existing weights, and [`LPSolver.PurgeSlackCuts`](../simplex.go) compacts inactive `extraRows` whose basic slack exceeds a threshold while preserving primal values, dual multipliers, and DSE weights on kept rows.
2. **Pivot row** (`ComputeTableauRow`): computes $\rho = B^{-T} e_r$ via `BTRANSparseHint(rho, {r}, rhoIdx)`, then accumulates $\bar a_{r} = \rho^T [A \; I]$ over `rhoIdx`. When the total structural nonzeros across touched rows stay below 64, `ComputeTableauRow` tracks the nonbasic support in `tabRowIdx`; otherwise it tracks touched 64-column blocks (`tabRowChunks`) and binary-searches each CSR row for its longest contiguous column prefix of length 32 or more to dispatch `axpy64`.
3. **Ratio test and Bound-Flipping Ratio Test (`selectDualBFRT`)**: when at least `minDualRatioSIMDChunks = 4` (256) structural columns are touched in `tabRowChunks`, `dualRatioPass1` invokes [`ScanDualRatioCand64With`](../internal/simd/kernels_amd64.go) to batch-collect candidate column indices (`<= 128` per call) using vector sign, pivot-tolerance, status, and Harris bound screening, skipping `d <= 0` divisions bit-exactly before updating $\theta_{\max} = \min (|d_k| + \epsilon_H) / |\bar a_{r,k}|$ with $\epsilon_H = \min(10^{-9}, 0.01 \cdot \text{effDualTol})$; Pass 2 picks, among candidates within $\theta_{\max}$, the largest pivot magnitude ($|\bar a_{r,k}|$ for $n \le 100$, or column-normalized $|\bar a_{r,k}| \cdot \text{feasTol}(k)$ for $n > 100$), preferring columns whose bound span absorbs the leaving row's violation and breaking near-ties (`1e-11`) by minimum basis row load (`colBasisLoad` via `rowBasicDeg` on non-degenerate steps, or static column degree and index on degenerate steps). When the Pass-2 winner is boxed, cannot absorb the violation alone, and at least one candidate has a positive dual step (`ratio > 1e-7`), `selectDualBFRT` sorts breakpoints and flips boxed nonbasic variables not in equality rows (`!colHasEq[k]`, up to 64 per pivot) to their opposite bounds, updating $x_B$ via one batched `FTRANSparse(tau, tauIdx)`.
4. **Farkas-verified dual ray**: if no column is eligible after a fresh basis rebuild, [`verifyDualInfeasibleRow`](../simplex.go) evaluates the cone-projected Farkas certificate via `BTRANExact` and `verifyUnscaledFarkas` directly against original unscaled rows and bounds across all columns; only certified rays return `StatusInfeasible`, while unverified rays fall through to Phase I primal simplex.

### Two-Phase Primal Simplex
Phase I minimizes the sum of bound violations of basic variables with a composite cost; Phase II uses Dantzig pricing with a bound-weighted cleanup pass for large-span columns whose reduced costs sit below `effDualTol` yet carry $|d_j| (u_j - l_j) > \epsilon_p$. Before Phase I reports `StatusInfeasible`, [`verifyPhase1Infeasible`](../simplex.go) validates the Farkas hyperplane in original unscaled problem space via `BTRANExact` and `verifyUnscaledFarkas` (retrying once from a smallest-magnitude crash basis `reinitSmallestMagnitudeCrash` when uncertified). In Phase I or Phase II, an improving column blocked only by sub-pivot entries ($0 < |d_i| \le 10^{-8}$, `pivotTol`) returns `StatusNumericalError` rather than a false `StatusUnbounded`. Both loops honor `LPSolver.MaxIter` and `LPSolver.Deadline`.

## 3. Multi-Tier SIMD Kernels (`internal/simd`)

Under the `simd` build experiment on `amd64`, a single portable `CGO_ENABLED=0` binary dispatches at runtime across three tiers based on CPUID feature detection (`archsimd.X86.AVX512()`, `AVX2()`, and `FMA()`): **AVX-512** ([`kernels_avx512_amd64.go`](../internal/simd/kernels_avx512_amd64.go), [`assign_avx512_amd64.go`](../internal/simd/assign_avx512_amd64.go)), **AVX2** ([`kernels_amd64.go`](../internal/simd/kernels_amd64.go), [`assign_amd64.go`](../internal/simd/assign_amd64.go)), and **4-way scalar** ([`kernels_scalar.go`](../internal/simd/kernels_scalar.go), [`assign_scalar.go`](../internal/simd/assign_scalar.go)), with runtime capping available via `SetMaxTier` (`auto`, `avx512`, `avx2`, `scalar`).

- **Kernel coverage and workload-aware thresholds**:
  - **LP & dense kernels**: `Dot64`, `Axpy64`, `Scale64`, `AbsMax64`, `AllAbsLE64With`, `RowActivityBoundsFinite`, `SelectDualLeaveRow64With`, `ScanDualRatioCand64With`, `CollectLE64With`, and `FillIntWith`. AVX2 activates at `minAVX2Len = 32` (`minLeaveRowAVX2 = 32`, `minDualRatioSIMDChunks = 4` = 256 cols). Because sparse LP loops spend most time in scalar LU/FTRAN/BTRAN where 512-bit instructions trigger Intel Emerald Rapids AVX-512 frequency down-binning, LP AVX-512 dispatch is gated at `minAVX512LPLen = 2048`, `minLeaveRowAVX512 = 2048`, and `minDualRatioAVX512Chunks = 128` (8,192 cols). Below vector thresholds, `Axpy64Scalar` executes four independent scalar accumulators to avoid read-after-write latency stalls on Intel Broadwell (`e2`).
  - **LSAP kernels**: `MinReducedSlice64With`, `InitReducedRow64`, `Min2ReducedRow64`, `RelaxRow64`, `MinNoNaN64`, `ColReduceRows64`, `ColReduceValidateRows64`, `Transpose64`, and `ValidateAndNegate64`. Dense LSAP inner loops spend >70% of runtime inside vector sweeps, so AVX-512 activates early at `minAVX512Len = 64` (`minAVX512Min2Len = 128`, vs. `minAVX2Min2Len = 256`).
- **Microarchitectural transition cleanup (`StoreArray` + `VZEROUPPER`)**: Go 1.27's `GOEXPERIMENT=simd` emits 256-bit VEX `YMM` and 512-bit EVEX `ZMM` instructions while emitting legacy non-VEX SSE instructions (`MOVSD`, `ADDSD`, `MULSD`, `SUBSD`, `MINSD`, `UCOMISD`) for scalar `float64` operations. Returning from a vector loop or executing scalar tail operations with dirty upper `YMM`/`ZMM` bits incurs an ~80 to 85 ns AVX-to-SSE state-transition stall per boundary, and extracting lanes via `GetLo()`/`GetHi()`/`GetElem()` adds cross-domain stalls while allowing the SSA scheduler to reorder `ClearAVXUpperBits()` above lane extraction. Every `go:noinline` AVX2 and AVX-512 kernel therefore isolates its `YMM`/`ZMM` register live ranges, spills vector accumulators via `StoreArray` before calling `archsimd.ClearAVXUpperBits()` (`VZEROUPPER`), and reduces lanes in scalar registers. Disassembly verification confirms zero EVEX instructions are hoisted into non-AVX-512 paths even under `GOAMD64=v1` and `GOAMD64=v3`.
- **Sign-symmetric rounding in `Axpy64` and dual-accumulator FMA in `Dot64`**: `Dot64` uses dual vector FMA accumulators (`MulAdd`) to hide FMA latency, whereas `Axpy64` uses separate vector multiply and add instructions, and `MinReducedSlice64With`, `InitReducedRow64`, `Min2ReducedRow64`, `SelectDualLeaveRow64With`, and `ScanDualRatioCand64With` mirror exact IEEE-754 scalar rounding and `NaN` propagation so scalar, AVX2, and AVX-512 paths remain bit-identical.
- **Reach and Amdahl's-law boundary**: in the LP solver, AVX2/AVX-512 kernels cover dense tableau row accumulation (`ComputeTableauRow`, dense rows for $n \le 256$ and contiguous CSR blocks of length $\ge 32$ for $n > 256$), dual leaving-row pricing (`SelectDualLeaveRow64With`), batched dual ratio-test screening (`ScanDualRatioCand64With`), and dual multiplier/reduced-cost updates. In `goblin/assign`, vector kernels cover entry validation/negation, cache-tiled transpose, column/row reduction preprocessing, and branchless Dijkstra relaxation (`RelaxRow64` + `MinNoNaN64`). Kernel crossover sweeps and paired solver comparisons (including GCP `n4` Emerald Rapids and `e2` Broadwell verification) are in [`benchmarks.md`](benchmarks.md#5-default-build-vs-goexperimentsimd).

## 4. MIP Presolve (`presolve.go`)

[`presolveWithDeadline`](../presolve.go) runs on MILPs before the root LP and checks the deadline between passes:
1. **GCD reduction**: on rows whose variables are integral and whose domain-weighted coefficient rounding error $\sum_j |a_{ij} - \operatorname{round}(a_{ij})| \max(|l_j|, |u_j|)$ stays at most $10^{-9}$, divides by $g = \gcd_j |\operatorname{round}(a_{ij})| > 1$ and rounds the error-relaxed right-hand side ($\lfloor b_i/g \rfloor$ for $\le$, $\lceil b_i/g \rceil$ for $\ge$, scaling `presolveIntTol` and `presolveFeasTol` by $1/g$ so large common divisors never mask small fractional infeasibilities). With $g = 1$ it still rounds integral rows' right-hand sides. The outer `SolveCompiled` entry point also verifies every returned vector against the unreduced `CompiledProblem` before returning.
2. **Activity bound tightening** (up to `maxPasses` rounds): [`runActivityTighteningDirty`](../presolve.go) tracks dirty rows touched by tightened columns and skips zero-bound terms and single-infinite-bound rows (`evalRowActivitySummary`, `fastRowCanSkip`) while deriving valid bounds from residual minimum and maximum row activities and rounding integer bounds.
3. **Redundant row and fixed-zero pruning**: drops `LessEqual` rows with $\overline{\alpha}_i \le b_i$, `GreaterEqual` rows with $\underline{\alpha}_i \ge b_i$, and all-fixed `Equal` rows satisfying $b_i$ (within $10^{-9}$), and strips fixed-zero ($l_j = u_j = 0$) entries from surviving rows.
4. **Coefficient strengthening**: in a $\le$ row with maximum activity $\overline{\alpha}_i > b_i$, a binary with $a_{ij} > 0$ and $\overline{\alpha}_i - a_{ij} < b_i$ gets $a_{ij}$ and $b_i$ both reduced by $\delta = b_i - (\overline{\alpha}_i - a_{ij})$; a binary with $a_{ij} < 0$ and $\overline{\alpha}_i + a_{ij} < b_i$ gets $a_{ij}$ increased by $b_i - (\overline{\alpha}_i + a_{ij})$.
5. **Verified $S_G$ orbit symmetry breaking** (`applyOrbitSymmetryBreaking`): seeds candidate $G$-way variable orbits from partitioning and packing rows, expands orbits across adjacent rows by degree, category, bounds, and coefficient signatures, and verifies exact constraint-matrix invariance under every adjacent group transposition $(g, g+1)$ via $O(G \cdot \text{rowLen})$ zero-sort verification on tuples with at most one unassigned column and packed `orbitSortEntry` keys otherwise (`proposeAndVerifyOrbits`). Under a group-invariant objective, a $G \times G$ binary subarray constrained by both row-wise group equations and column-wise orbit partitioning or packing equations is fixed to the identity $I_G$ (followed by another activity-tightening pass), whereas general partitioning or packing binary orbits fix upper-triangular orbitope entries $x_{k_r, g} = 0$ for $g > r$ (and $x_{k_0, 0} = 1$ on equalities). Under a monotonically non-decreasing group objective where $l_v \ge 0$ and a binary indicator $y_g = 0$ forces all cost-varying variables in group $g$ to zero, presolve appends empty-block ordering inequalities $y_g - y_{g+1} \ge 0$.

Node-level propagation (`propagateBounds`) applies the activity rules to each node's bounds when presolve is enabled.

## 5. Cutting Planes (`cuts.go`)

At the root, [`generateCutRound`](../cuts.go) separates four families, keeps candidates with efficacy at least $10^{-3}$ and coefficient dynamism at most $10^5$, rejects candidates whose inner-product cosine exceeds $0.92$ against an already chosen cut, and adds up to $\min(60, \max(15, m/3))$ cuts per round as one batch. Every cut is normalized and its right-hand side relaxed by $10^{-7}$. Coefficients of magnitude at most $10^{-11}$ are dropped only after adding the largest possible value of the dropped term over the variable's global bounds to the right-hand side; a cut whose dropped term is unbounded is rejected. If the LP fails to re-solve to an optimal basis after a round (infeasible, pivot or time limit, numerical error), the round is rolled back to the last optimal LP state and cutting stops. Trailing zero-gain cut rounds are rolled back to the last bound-improving anchor snapshot, and cuts whose normalized basic slack exceeds $0.05$ at the end of the root loop are purged via [`LPSolver.PurgeSlackCuts`](../simplex.go) before branch-and-bound.

1. **Gomory mixed-integer cuts** from up to 25 tableau rows whose basic integer variable has fractional part $f_0 \in [0.01, 0.99]$. With nonbasic variables shifted to their active bounds ($\tilde x_j \ge 0$):
   $$\sum_{j \in \mathcal{I}: f_j \le f_0} \frac{f_j}{f_0} \tilde{x}_j + \sum_{j \in \mathcal{I}: f_j > f_0} \frac{1 - f_j}{1 - f_0} \tilde{x}_j + \sum_{j \in \mathcal{C}: \bar{a}_j > 0} \frac{\bar{a}_j}{f_0} \tilde{x}_j - \sum_{j \in \mathcal{C}: \bar{a}_j < 0} \frac{\bar{a}_j}{1 - f_0} \tilde{x}_j \ge 1$$
   Integer columns with fractional active bounds, and all slacks, are treated as continuous.
2. **Lifted knapsack covers**: on each inequality row relaxed to a binary knapsack (non-binary terms moved to their worst bound, negative binaries complemented), finds a minimal cover $C$ and lifts the remaining items sequentially with a dynamic program over the minimum weight needed to reach each cut activity, giving $\sum_{j \in C} x_j + \sum_{j \notin C} \alpha_j x_j \le |C| - 1$.
3. **VUB disaggregation and $(l, S)$ inequalities**: from rows $\sum_k a_k x_k - C y \le 0$ with $x_k \ge 0$ and binary $y$, derives $x_k \le \min(u_k, C / a_k)\, y$; from chains of flow-balance rows $s_{t-1} + x_t - s_t = d_t$ with VUB-linked production, separates $s_{t-1} + \sum_{\tau \in [t,q] \setminus S} x_\tau + \sum_{\tau \in S} d_{\tau q} y_\tau \ge d_{tq}$.
4. **Single-row mixed-integer rounding**: on inequality rows without free $(-\infty, \infty)$ variables, shifts each boxed variable to its nearer bound and each one-sided variable ($[l_j, \infty)$ or $(-\infty, u_j]$) to its finite bound (`mirUseLower`), divides by $\delta = |a_{ik}|$ for up to four fractional integer variables, and applies the MIR function. No multi-row combinations are formed.

## 6. Primal Heuristics and Branch-and-Cut (`heuristics.go`, `bab.go`)

### Primal Heuristics
- **Propagated rounding** (`tryPropagatedRounding`): fixes already-integral columns in one linear pass and sorts only truly fractional columns in order of increasing fractional part with queue-based bound tightening (`propagateFromCol`), trying nearest and objective-directed rounding while deduplicating identical integer fixings between strategies. On mixed models with more than 32 fractional integer columns it still runs closed-form epigraph and single-row completion, skipping only the general sub-LP fallback. When root cuts fail to improve the LP relaxation bound on round 1, `runRootCutLoop` exits immediately, rolls back to the root anchor, and runs deferred diving and zero-cut sub-MIP heuristics.
- **Fixed-integer LP** (`solveFixedIntContinuousLP`): with integer decisions fixed, tightens bounds via two passes of `propagateBounds`, solves L1/epigraph continuous and rounding-linked subproblems in closed form (`solveEpigraphContinuousLP`, decoupling $\beta = 0$ and $\beta \ne 0$ paths in `solveEpigraphComponent` with stack buffers and error-bounded `float64` screening before exact `dot2Acc`) as well as separable single-row bounded continuous knapsacks, and otherwise re-solves the continuous part warm-started on `heurSolver` (which skips redundant `InitCrashBasis` when cloning the root basis) or as a compact LP over the free columns.
- **Root sub-MIP** (`tryRootSubMIP`): with 1 to 120 fractional integers at the root on models with equality constraints or continuous-linked integer columns (`findContLinkedIntCols`), frees the integer columns of short equality rows that contain a fractional variable (or two basic variables when $n \le 200$), fixes the rest, and requires at least 25% fixed integers when cuts tightened the root (or at least 50% fixed integers under the zero-cut RINS gate when `!enableCuts`). The reduced model is solved via `solveBranchAndCutWithDone` (using an isolated pooled `Workspace` and `LPSolver`, capped by the remaining `MaxNodes` and `MaxIter` budgets and accounting all explored sub-MIP nodes and pivots back into the parent search) seeded with the outer incumbent cutoff (at most 250 nodes, stopping early after 48 nodes without incumbent improvement, and at most 1.5 s). Before branch-and-bound begins, `tightenUnboundedIntCols` propagates objective-implied upper and lower bounds from the incumbent onto unbounded integer columns so node relaxations remain boxed and dual-feasible across branch switches.
- **Fractional diving** (`tryFractionalDiving`): up to 18 steps of fixing a fractional variable from pure-integer equality rows, propagating, and re-solving warm-started with `fastHeuristicPrimal` enabled on intermediate diving LPs (keeping exact verification on the final incumbent).

### Branching and Search
- **Branching column choice** (`selectBranchVar`): pseudocost product scores $\max(f \bar\psi^-, 10^{-4}) \cdot \max((1-f) \bar\psi^+, 10^{-4})$. At depth 2 or less it strong-branches on up to 5 unreliable candidates with 25 dual pivots per child. Only optimal child solves update pseudocosts; an infeasible child scores high; a pivot-limited child falls back to its pseudocost estimate.
- **Pseudocosts**: each node records its branching side and step length. Gains are stored per unit of step length; steps shorter than $10^{-3}$ are ignored.
- **Node choice**: best bound with plunging into the preferred child, switching to the queue every 8 nodes. Child nodes link to their parent with one `boundDelta` in $O(1)$ time and compact ancestor chains into deduplicated column bounds every 128 steps.

### Incumbents and Near-Integral Nodes
An LP point is near-integral when every integer variable is within $10^{-6}$ of an integer. After snapping integer variables (and re-solving continuous columns via `polishIncumbent` when continuous residuals exceed $10^{-7}$, `primalTol`), a candidate that violates no bound, row, or integer requirement by more than $10^{-6}$ updates the incumbent if improving. Crucially, on big-M formulations a near-integral LP point ($y_j \approx 10^{-7}$) can polish to a feasible yet suboptimal integer solution whose objective sits well above the LP bound; therefore the root or node fathoms only when `isGapClosed` holds against that LP relaxation bound, and otherwise branches via `selectNearIntegralBranchVar` on the non-fixed integer variable furthest from an integer. A node that cannot be branched keeps its LP bound in the global bound and makes the final status `StatusNumericalError` rather than `StatusOptimal`.

### Limits and Bounds
- `TimeLimit`, `MaxNodes`, and `MaxIter` (total main-tree and root sub-MIP simplex pivots in `Result.Iterations`) stop the search. A node that could not be evaluated because of a limit goes back to the queue, so its bound stays in the global bound.
- A node LP that fails twice (warm start, then slack basis) keeps its parent bound in the global bound.
- **The `BestBound` field**: the minimum over open nodes, gap-pruned nodes, unresolved nodes, and the incumbent; at an early root exit it is bounded by the root LP or root cut bound. Reporting `StatusOptimal` requires the gap between `BestBound` and the incumbent to close within `RelGapTol`/`AbsGapTol`.

## 7. Linear Sum Assignment (`goblin/assign`)

The subpackage [`goblin/assign`](../assign/solver.go) solves the $m \times n$ Linear Sum Assignment Problem (`LSAP`) by maintaining dual potentials $u \in \mathbb{R}^m$ and $v \in \mathbb{R}^n$ that satisfy complementary slackness on the normalized dimensions $(m, n) = (\min(\text{rows}, \text{cols}), \max(\text{rows}, \text{cols}))$:

1. **Single-Pass Validation, Minimal Exponent Scaling, and Cache-Tiled Transpose** (`validateAndPrepareCost`): validates finite entries and forbidden $+\infty$ (or $-\infty$ when `sense == Maximize`) costs in one pass. When `rows > cols`, the matrix is transposed in $32 \times 32$ tiles into `ws.workCost`; when `sense == Maximize`, finite costs are negated in the same pass. If any finite entry exceeds $10^{290}$ in magnitude, `minimalDownscaleExp` selects only the smallest power-of-two downscale exponent needed to keep $16 n \cdot \max |C_{ij}|$ below the finite `float64` ceiling so intermediate differences $(C_{i, j} - v_j) - u_i$ cannot overflow; if downscaling rounds or underflows any subnormal entry (`lostSubnormal`), or if `hasPotentialCancellation` detects cross-scale `float64` potential cancellation (such as $\pm 10^{17}$ potentials masking unit cost differences), a second exact residual LSAP stage resolves ties over `twoSumAssign` reduced residuals (~106-bit effective potential dynamic range). The returned objective unconditionally accumulates via stack-backed multi-scale Shewchuk expansions (`twoSumAssign`), and dual potentials keep $v_j = 0$ on unassigned rectangular columns while shifting overflowing square potentials into the finite interval intersection before clamping and re-projecting for complementary slackness.
2. **Dual Preprocessing**:
   - **Square ($m = n$, [`preprocessSquareJV`](../assign/solver.go))**: executes Jonker-Volgenant (1987) row-major column reduction and reduction transfer over uniquely matched rows. If nearly all rows remain unassigned (`numFree >= n - max(2, n/32)`), [`isCollinearSquare`](../assign/solver.go) tests whether column increments across unassigned rows are sign-concordant: collinear (rank-1 or Monge) matrices run a greedy-chain warm start ordered by extreme-column spread $C_{i, j_{\max}} - C_{i, j_{\min}}$, whereas non-collinear row-dominated matrices normalize row scales via $u_i = \min_j C_{i, j}$ and repeat column reduction and reduction transfer on $C_{i, j} - u_i$. Finally, [`augmentingRowReduction`](../assign/solver.go) executes two passes of chain-following augmenting row reduction.
   - **Rectangular ($m < n$, [`preprocessRectangularBV`](../assign/solver.go))**: executes Bijsterbosch-Volgenant (2010) row reduction keeping $v_j = 0$ on unassigned columns (`row4col[j] < 0`), followed by two passes of [`augmentingRowReduction`](../assign/solver.go).
3. **3-Way Partitioned Shortest Augmenting Path** ([`augmentRowScalar`](../assign/solver.go) and [`augmentRowSIMD`](../assign/solver.go)): for each remaining unassigned row $i^*$, initializes $d_j = (C_{i^*, j} - v_j) - u_{i^*}$ and $\min = \min_j d_j$ via [`simd.InitReducedRow64With`](../internal/simd/kernels_amd64.go), returning immediately (`1-hop` fast path) if an unassigned column (`row4col[j] == -1`) achieves $d_j = \min$. In scalar mode (`augmentRowScalar`), it maintains the Jonker-Volgenant 3-way column permutation `remaining[0:low]` (permanently labeled), `remaining[low:up]` (current minimum-$d$ frontier), and `remaining[up:n]` (unscanned) in lockstep with `d[0:n]`, using a 4-way unrolled scan over `d[up:n]` when `low == up` and relaxing outgoing edges across `remaining[up:n]`. Under `GOEXPERIMENT=simd` (`augmentRowSIMD`, $n \ge 32$), it indexes `d[0:n]` directly by column with `NaN` sentinels for queued columns (`dQueue[0:up]` saving frontier distances), relaxing all columns branchlessly via 4-wide AVX2 or 8-wide AVX-512 [`simd.RelaxRow64`](../internal/simd/assign_amd64.go) (using `minAVX512Len = 64`) and finding the next minimum via [`simd.MinNoNaN64`](../internal/simd/assign_amd64.go). Dual potentials then update in $O(\text{low})$ over `remaining[0:low]`, and the primal matching augments along `pred`.

## Known Limits of the Pure-Go Engine

- **LP updates and primal fallback**: while the basis solver uses a three-stage singleton and sparse Markowitz LU with `FTRANSparseHint`/`BTRANSparseHint`, `BTRANExact` Farkas verification, conditional power-of-two Ruiz equilibration, and dual bound flipping (`selectDualBFRT`), basis updates between full factor rebuilds use a product-form (PFI) eta file rather than Forrest-Tomlin $U$-updates, and the Phase II primal fallback uses Dantzig pricing with a Bland anti-cycling fallback.
- **Cuts at the root only**: cuts are separated at the root node using global bounds (with slack root cuts purged before branching), without in-tree cut generation or multi-row aggregation.
- **Reference solver**: parity is checked against COIN-OR CBC (`c1c171ec`) across the 293-instance synthetic corpus and 20 held-out public Netlib LP and MIPLIB 3.0 MILP instances solved to optimality plus a 25-node dual-bound check on `coinor_capplan1` ([`public_benchmarks_test.go`](../public_benchmarks_test.go)); `bench/difftest_cbc.py` verifies mismatches independently before blaming either solver.
